Creatures of the Deep

Evolution's strangest experiments live far below the waves.

The Anglerfish

Perhaps the most iconic deep-sea predator, the anglerfish lures prey with a bioluminescent appendage dangling from its head — a biological fishing rod tipped with glowing bacteria. In the pitch black of the abyss, smaller fish are drawn helplessly toward the light.

Female anglerfish can stretch their massive jaws and expand their stomachs to swallow prey twice their own size. The males, by contrast, are tiny parasites — they latch onto a female and physically fuse with her body, sharing her bloodstream for the rest of their lives.

Scientists didn't photograph a living anglerfish in its natural habitat until 2014. They are thought to live between 200 and 2,000 meters below the surface, in conditions that would kill a human instantly without protection.

Quick Facts

  • Size: Females up to 1.2m; males only a few centimeters
  • Depth: 200 – 2,000 meters
  • Lifespan: Estimated 25+ years
  • Diet: Fish, crustaceans, squid
  • Special ability: Bioluminescent lure (esca)
  • Threat level: Harmless to humans

More Denizens of the Dark

Giant Squid

Reaching up to 13 meters in length, the giant squid was considered a myth for centuries. It possesses the largest eyes of any living animal — each as large as a dinner plate — to detect the faintest traces of light in the deep.

Immortal Jellyfish

Turritopsis dohrnii is the only known animal capable of reverting to its juvenile state after reaching adulthood — making it biologically immortal. It is found at various ocean depths worldwide.

Dumbo Octopus

Named for its ear-like fins that resemble the Disney elephant, the dumbo octopus lives deeper than any other known octopus species — up to 7,000 meters. It hovers above the seafloor like a ghost, hunting worms and crustaceans.
